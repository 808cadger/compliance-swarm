# Pre-Payroll Compliance Checklist

Run through this before approving any pay run in your actual payroll provider. This is a
verification pass, not a substitute for one — the payroll agent worksheet feeds into this,
it does not replace it.

## Every pay period

- [ ] Every non-exempt employee's overtime (>40 hrs/week) is calculated at 1.5x, not folded
into regular pay
- [ ] No exempt employee's hours look like they're being used to justify skipping overtime
they're actually owed (misclassification risk — see flag notes below)
- [ ] Every hourly rate is at or above the *current, verified* state and local minimum wage
(the payroll agent's minimum wage figures are illustrative demo values — always check
the state labor department's current posted rate, not this template)
- [ ] Tipped employees (if any) meet minimum wage after tip credit is applied, per your state's
specific tip credit rules
- [ ] New hires have a completed I-9 and W-4 (or state equivalent) on file before their first
paycheck
- [ ] Any employee whose hours look unusually high (60+ in a week) has been double-checked
against time records, not just trusted as data entry

## Quarterly

- [ ] Payroll tax deposits (federal 941, state withholding) are current — check your deposit
schedule (semi-weekly vs. monthly depends on prior-period liability size)
- [ ] State unemployment insurance (SUTA) filing is current
- [ ] Workers' comp coverage is active and premium basis matches actual payroll run — insurers
audit this annually and shortfalls get billed retroactively

## Annually

- [ ] W-2s issued to employees and 1099-NECs issued to contractors by the January deadline
- [ ] Any employee who crossed a state line (remote work, travel) has been checked for
multi-state withholding obligations
- [ ] Minimum wage table (used by the payroll agent) has been updated for the new year — most
states adjust January 1

## Misclassification red flags worth a second look

- [ ] Exempt-classified employees regularly logging 45+ hours with no extra pay
- [ ] Contractors (1099) who work set hours, use company equipment, or take direction like an
employee — this is an IRS/DOL audit magnet regardless of what the contract says
- [ ] Any worker paid a flat weekly amount regardless of hours worked, without a documented
exemption basis (executive, administrative, professional, or outside sales duties test)

---
This checklist covers common triggers, not a complete list of federal or state
obligations. Treat it as a first pass — a licensed payroll professional or CPA should
review your actual setup at least once before you rely on this process unsupervised.
